Online Students

Same jobs, same panel characters, same feedback, on your own schedule. You have two weeks.

You’ll be in a pod of about 4 classmates (usually 4, sometimes 3 or 5), set up as a group in Canvas. Your pod has two weeks to finish. The dates are in Canvas.

Start with the same before-class prep as everyone else, including the Pre-Class Discussion post, and do it before your pod meets. Then pick the option your pod can make work. In both, panelists get their questions from the Interview room on the candidate’s job page (see Meet the Panel).

  • Option 1: Self-scheduled live pod (recommended)
  • Option 2: Async video panel (if your pod can’t find a time)

Your pod finds one ~50-minute block sometime in the two-week window and runs the full activity on a video call. It’s the same activity the in-person class does, so it’s the closest thing to a real panel interview.

Step 1: Find a time (first few days)

  • Post your availability in your Canvas group as early as you can. A quick poll (for example, When2meet) works well.
  • If your instructor posts Canvas Calendar → Scheduler slots, you can sign up for one of those instead, up to 4 people per slot.
  • Aim to have a time booked by the end of week 1. That leaves room to reschedule if something comes up.

Step 2: Meet (any time in the two weeks)

  1. Meet on Zoom, Teams, or Google Meet. Allow about 50 minutes for a pod of 4, or 60 for a pod of 5.
  2. Number off 1, 2, 3, … and rename yourself with your number, for example 1 – Alex.
  3. Choose a timekeeper. They open the Timer, set the pod size, click Start now, and paste the link in the call chat. Everyone opens it. The timer is time-zone-safe and shows the same countdown everywhere.
  4. Run the rounds as described on the Instructions page, using the rotation table for your pod size. Each panelist opens the candidate’s job page → Interview room → their own seat in a separate window, and draws a new mood card each round.
  5. Pod of 3? That’s fine. Use the A+B Hiring Manager seat.
  6. Cameras on, if you can. Video interviews are a normal part of hiring now.

Use this option if your pod truly can’t find a shared hour. It runs entirely through your Canvas group discussion, and nobody needs to be online at the same time.

Each pod member gets one thread where they’re the candidate. On every other thread, you’re on the panel. This format matches the one-way video interviews many employers use for first-round screens.

How the two weeks run

When Candidate (your own thread) Panelist (your podmates’ threads)
Days 1–3 Start your thread, titled Candidate: Your Name – Job Title. Post the job link and a 2–3 sentence “cover note” on why you’re applying. Nothing yet.
Days 3–5 Don’t read the questions until you’re ready to record. Post one question in character for each podmate. Use your assigned Seat and a fresh mood card.
Days 5–9 Record one video, 8–10 minutes: your 60-second pitch, then each panelist’s question, then one question for the panel. Nothing yet.
Days 9–11 Nothing yet. Watch the video. Reply with one follow-up in your seat’s style, plus a short in-character answer to the candidate’s question.
Days 11–14 Answer each follow-up in a short reply, either a video of 2 minutes or less or about 150 words of text. Drop character and post your three debrief notes: a hired-peer moment, a hedge or too-academic moment, and one thing to try.

Which seat do I play? Use the rotation table for your pod size. Each round corresponds to one podmate’s thread: Person 1’s thread is Round 1, Person 2’s thread is Round 2, and so on.

Posting your panel question

  • A 30–60 second video clip is best. Text is fine too.
  • Introduce yourself in character, for example “Hi, I’m Pat, chair of the search committee. Apologies, it’s been a morning.”
  • Open the candidate’s job page → Interview room → your seat, and pick one of that seat’s questions. Draw a mood card from the same page and let it shape how you ask, without naming the card.

Recording your candidate video

  • Read the questions only when you’re ready to record, and give yourself about 30 seconds to think before each answer.
  • Record in one take if you can, the way you would for a real one-way interview. One restart is fine, but don’t polish it.
  • Use Canvas’s built-in Record/Upload Media button in the reply editor, or record a Zoom meeting with just you.

If a panelist goes quiet: don’t wait for them. If a podmate hasn’t posted by Day 5, pick one of their seat’s questions from your job page’s Interview room yourself, and say which seat it came from.

Video-interview tips

  • Put your camera at eye level, and look at the camera when you’re making a key point.
  • Light your face from the front with a window or lamp. Avoid light coming from behind you.
  • Notes on a sticky note next to the camera are fine. Reading a script off the screen is noticeable.
  • Live calls: unmute, pause for a beat, then answer, because lag can make people talk over each other.
